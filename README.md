## Four mistakes that cost beginners more than the platform choice

**Funding with a debit or credit card.** NerdWallet's guidance is to connect a bank account by ACH and fund directly. A 3.99% card fee on $2,600 of buying is about $104 a year for nothing.

**Judging a platform by its headline fee while the simple quote hides a spread.** Check the amount of crypto that lands in your account against the spot price at that moment. That difference is your real cost.

**Using SMS for two-factor authentication.** Use an authenticator app instead. SIM swaps are a known route into exchange accounts.

**Assuming exchange balances are FDIC-insured.** Only US dollar cash held at partner banks is covered. Crypto isn't, and in a bankruptcy you'd be a creditor waiting in line, which is where FTX and BlockFi customers spent years from 2022. Move holdings you'd hate to lose into a wallet where you hold the recovery phrase.

## The tax part most beginner guides skip

Buying crypto with dollars is not a taxable event in the US. Selling, swapping one coin for another, or spending crypto is, and the gain is your proceeds minus cost basis including fees; hold longer than a year and the lower long-term rates apply. Starting with tax year 2025, US exchanges report your sales to the IRS on Form 1099-DA, but the 2025 form shows gross proceeds without cost basis. Full cost-basis reporting only starts with assets bought on or after 1 January 2026 and held at the same exchange the whole time. Keep your own records from the first purchase and you won't have to reconstruct them later.
